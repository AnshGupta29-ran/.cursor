### What's missing from every personal finance app?

### Novel Feature Ideas

**1. Spending Velocity Warning System**
Most apps show you spent $X this month. But the real insight is *velocity* — are you spending faster than usual this week? A sudden spike on Tuesday that's 2x your normal Tuesday spending is an early warning, not a post-mortem.
- **Implementation**: Compare current week's daily spending rate vs. trailing 3-month average for same day-of-week
- **UX**: A small orange/red indicator in the header when velocity exceeds normal range

**2. "Runway" Calculator (context-aware)**
Not just "you have $5,000." But: "At your current spending rate, including upcoming bills you've entered, you have 47 days of runway."
- **Implementation**: Take current balance, subtract upcoming/recurring expenses, divide by average daily spend
- **Differentiator**: Most tools show this for businesses (burn rate). Personal finance almost never does.
- **UX**: A prominent card showing days of runway, color-coded (green >60 days, yellow 30-60, red <30)

**3. Category Anomaly Detection**
"When you spent $200 on dining out this week, that's 3.2x your normal weekly dining spend."
- **Implementation**: For each category, track weekly spending and flag when current week exceeds 2 standard deviations from mean
- **UX**: "Anomalies" section — small cards with explanations, not just raw numbers

**4. "What-If" Scenario Simulator**
"What if I put an extra $200/month toward savings?"
- **Implementation**: Simple projection model that takes current trends and applies a hypothetical change
- **UX**: A slider-based interface in analytics — "move this slider to see the effect on your 6-month outlook"
- **Note**: This is usually a premium feature in apps like YNAB. Including it for free is a differentiator.

**5. Recurring Transaction Auto-Detection**
If you see "Netflix" for $15.99 every month, suggest marking it as recurring.
- **Implementation**: Simple heuristic — same description + same amount appears in multiple months
- **UX**: "We noticed 3 transactions that look recurring — want to mark them?" with one-click accept

**6. Friction Log (Transaction Effort Tracking)**
Every time you log a transaction, track *how long it sat in your head* before you logged it. The friction of logging correlates with spending you're less proud of.
- **Implementation**: Optionally timestamp when a transaction happened vs. when it was logged. Compute lag time.
- **UX**: Subtle insight — "You logged dining expenses 4 days late on average, but logged income the same day"

**7. Spending Personality Snapshot**
"Your spending pattern most resembles: **The Stabilizer** — predictable expenses, steady savings, occasional splurge weekends."
- **Implementation**: Simple decision tree based on: income volatility, expense volatility, savings rate consistency, category diversity
- **UX**: Fun, shareable card in the analytics section. A conversation starter.

**8. Dark Patterns Detector**
Identify subscriptions you forgot about — the $3.99/month charge you haven't thought about in 8 months.
- **Implementation**: Flag transactions from the same vendor that occur monthly with no active engagement
- **UX**: "You've spent $47.88 on [service] this year without any other interaction"
